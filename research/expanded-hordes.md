# Expanded Hordes: implementation decision package

Read the [start-here handoff](expanded-hordes-handoff.md) first for the user's corrections, basic summary and decision list. This longer document is supporting research, not an implementation plan.

**Follow-up correction:** [composition research](expanded-hordes-composition.md) now verifies bosses in the actual native horde registry, their frequencies, all ten biome rosters and the existing cross-group factory support. It supersedes this report's unresolved boss-eligibility/mandatory-adapter claims. The [decision record](expanded-hordes-decisions.md) owns subsequent user choices and corrections, including use of a large finite budget and acceptance of native dawn timing. This document records the earlier research stage, not current scope approval.

Research: 2026-09-21, America/New_York. **Research only; no product choices, mod implementation, deployment, game launch, or save changes.**

The proposed budget/concurrency system is feasible. Vanilla already replenishes dead horde members until a finite total is exhausted. Its existing mechanisms expose several ways to separate total from concurrent population and customize a nighttime deadline. Their behavior, difficulty and remaining uncertainties are the subject of this research, not an implementation plan.

**Confirmed constraint:** custom timeframes remain inside vanilla nighttime; daytime hordes are out of scope. **Expressed preference:** work with vanilla mechanics as much as possible. That preference informs the tradeoffs, but does not select a patch boundary, implementation approach, or product behavior. The user will choose after reviewing the research and difficulty of the options. No implementation plan is approved.

## 1. Vanilla architecture and verified behavior

| Area | Current behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Schedule | `NPC_Horde_Mgr.Check_Horde_Events` waits for four surrounding terrains, then checks every 5 scaled seconds. Requires the game's nighttime flag: `Creature_Mgr.Is_Day_Time` defines daytime as strictly after 06:30 and before 19:00. The loaded world uses a seeded random delay of 1-6 hours after 19:00, hence 20:00 through 01:00. The next event is scheduled when the current event starts. [E1,E9] |
| Total and concurrency | World values: base total **15**, pioneers **2**, growth **2/event**. For wave `W` and multiplier `M`, total is `Round(15*M)+2*(W-1)`. Initial concurrent target is `min(remaining total, Round(2*M)+(W-1), AI limit)`. The loaded AI limit is **60**. At saved wave 17 and M=8 these calculate to **152 total, 32 concurrent**; this is not an observed completed battle. [E1,E2] |
| Replenishment | The spawn coroutine waits while living horde membership reaches its target. Death removes living membership immediately, before the corpse finishes settling, allowing a replacement. A successful registration increments total emitted. Vanilla attempts one serial spawn then waits 0.1 scaled seconds. Invalid locations retry; an asynchronous asset/spawn failure is not a successful budget debit. The finite loop can nevertheless finish short after such a failure. [E1,E3] |
| Location and composition | Uses the player's terrain biome, a cycling local NPC group, and that group's weighted outdoor prefab list. Initial anchor is 100-150 m away. Subsequent candidates must remain at least 40 m from the player. Native position validation can attempt 1,000 anchor or 100 subsequent samples, with multiple raycast passes. Floating-origin coordinates are converted for saves. [E1] |
| Tracking and focus | Separate dictionaries track spawned objects and living horde members. Dead ragdolls remain in the former until conversion. Horde objects have `_npcSpawnSource=2`. Spawn immediately focuses the player. AI focus allowance is shared with ambient zombies, not a horde-exclusive population cap. [E1,E2] |
| End and cleanup | Dawn stops further spawning; it does **not** kill surviving zombies. Exhausting total also only ends emission. A later event returns previous living horde members to the pool. AI distance handling can return horde enemies to the pool at roughly 500 m from their spawn/target reference. Native cleanup also maintains GPU prototypes, group reference counts, loot interaction and controller state. [E1,E2] |
| Save/load | Saves wave, emitted count, schedule, anchor, and surviving prefab/group/level/position records to `PNP/PNPH`. No survivor HP is stored; restoration creates full-health survivors. Restoration is entered only when at least one survivor exists. Daytime save/load clears survivor records, consistent with retaining vanilla's nighttime boundary. Saving during a zero-living nighttime refill interval needs a targeted extension if exact event continuation is chosen. [E1] |
| Existing strength scaling | Horde level is already the greater of distance-based level and a nearby-base measure. Distance is radial distance beyond 512 m, not the count of unique biomes. Base measure sums current `Shards_HP_All` of player-deployed, non-system-roof structures in four nearby terrains, then applies a saturating level curve capped toward 600. It is neither total world wealth nor material cost. [E1,E9] |

The native HUD reports the next arrival, without active budget/concurrency status. Death/respawn does not supply a new event policy: AI has delayed refocusing of existing horde members. Terrain/scene transitions and player escape need explicit handling in a continuous-event controller.

## 2. Core decisions

Each row is an independent choice unless its dependencies say otherwise. “Event ends” must distinguish stopping new spawns from disposing of enemies already present.

| Time limit | Configured start/end within 19:00-06:30; duration bounded by that night; native dawn end | Shorter custom window or the remaining vanilla night | L-M | Preserve the native day/night gate; handle midnight and clock jumps. Unlimited budget still stops at the chosen end or vanilla dawn | Longer events increase cumulative kills, corpse/loot pressure and save growth |
| Start schedule | Native interval/randomized start; configurable random range inside nighttime; fixed nighttime hour on the native interval | Unpredictable or predictable arrival | L-M | Extend native scheduling rather than run a competing trigger; handle loading after a missed start. Reject or clamp invalid hours explicitly | Negligible steady-state cost |
| Limit reached | Stop emission and leave survivors; stop and immediately pool survivors; stop then pool after a specified grace period | Fight remaining enemies, immediate relief, or limited cleanup time | L-M after controller | Immediate cleanup at budget exhaustion makes the final spawned enemies disappear; pooling must not award kills/loot | Leaving survivors retains their cost; pooling releases it |
| Concurrent population | Horde-only living cap; combined ambient+horde cap by withholding horde spawns; horde allocation within a combined cap | Reliable horde size, or reduced hordes when ambient population is high | M | Combined caps cannot guarantee a hard total while ambient spawns remain uncontrolled. A true hard cap needs an ambient admission policy too | Bounded living count is the main available CPU/physics control |
| Refill shape | Continuous serial top-up with configurable delay; timed batches/pulses with the same cap | Constant pressure or recovery intervals | M | Reserve pending slots; never fill from the spawned dictionary because it includes corpses | Rate limiting spreads creation, asset loading and path requests; simultaneous batches cause spikes |

| Spawn placement | Native directional cluster/radii; configurable valid ring; several validated directions | One advancing front or surrounding pressure | L-M | Keep collision/terrain checks and an explicit failure retry/backoff policy; unsuitable terrain cannot guarantee requested population | More candidate searches can hitch; bound work per frame rather than only number of enemies |

| Player death / unavailable terrain | Retain native clock/refocus behavior with guarded spawning; suspend replenishment while invalid; end event | Event continues, pressure pauses, or grants a reset | L-M | All choices retain the nighttime end. Additional state must not duplicate native survivor records | Save/load cost scales with survivors and retained loot records |
| Configuration and display | BepInEx config with changes applied next event; explicit mid-event application; optional active-event HUD | Predictable fixed event settings, live tuning, or visible budget/time/population | L-M; HUD M | Mid-event lower caps/budgets need a defined rule; third-party ModMenu integration remains unverified | Configuration negligible; HUD should use controller counters, not scene scans |

**Unselected implementation options:** targeted patches to the native scheduler/iterator could retain most vanilla orchestration (M difficulty, sensitivity to game updates). Replacing just its spawn coroutine could simplify unlimited replenishment and pending-slot accounting while retaining native scheduling/factory/tracking (M difficulty, more native sequencing to reproduce). A separate event controller is technically possible (M-H), but introduces additional synchronization with native scheduling and saves; no requirement identified here makes it necessary. No boundary is selected. The appropriate comparison depends on the user's chosen behavior, and a disposable patch proof remains outstanding.

**Necessary extensions depend on the choices:** retain vanilla emitted count, wave/schedule and living membership; add pending-slot accounting, the selected deadline, and protection against callbacks from an ended event. Add only the missing persistent fields if reliable zero-survivor continuation or other extra persistence is chosen. Scope stat changes to source 2 and reset pooled properties. Reconcile requested population with native AI limits and cleanup; add validation/backoff for failed spawn positions. A small companion save record can travel with slot directories because native `UI_Control.SyncDirectoryCore` recursively copies files. It still needs save-generation/slot identity and handling of missing records, since that copier does not by itself remove stale destination files. [E1,E2,E10]

Native spawning uses shared asynchronous flags and scratch state. Serial admission is necessary when reusing it unchanged. Stopping its coroutine does not cancel an already-running asynchronous factory call: late completions must be rejected or returned through native cleanup after a deadline, load or event change.

## 3. Corpse and loot lifecycle

There are **two separate retention systems**, not one corpse counter. [E3-E6]

1. A living zombie dies and frees its horde slot immediately. Its physical ragdoll remains temporarily. The loaded world has `_maxDeadRagdolls=4`, a conversion-pressure threshold rather than an instantaneous hard cap. Periodic checks use body motion, distance, visibility, elapsed time and backlog; mass deaths can temporarily exceed four.
2. Conversion creates a GPU-instanced static body and returns the original NPC through native pooling. Its loot interaction data is transferred from the ragdoll instance key to a persistent corpse key.
3. Static corpses activate near 120 m and hide beyond 150 m. Distance hiding keeps the saved corpse and loot; it is different from deletion.
4. On the usual existing-prefab spawn path, reaching `_MaxCorpseCount` evicts **one oldest active static corpse**. The first-use prefab path bypasses that check. Lowering the limit does not immediately trim everything. It is not a strict cap on every saved corpse or on all ragdolls.
5. Lifetime expiry (game days; zero disables that expiry), smashing a corpse, and destruction of supporting structure can also remove it. Permanent removal invokes the same cleanup event that deletes its owner/empty-container records. **No automatic ground-loot drop occurs on this path.**

Loot is rolled lazily on first opening. An unopened body usually represents a future loot opportunity, not an existing bag of items. The roll depends on loot settings, relevant player perks and quality initialization at the player's then-current location. Already-opened items carry quality, durability and other state that must transfer exactly. “Preserve loot” therefore has a generation-timing decision as well as a storage decision. [E5,E6]

| Decision | Viable options | Player effect | Difficulty | Risk | Performance impact |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Retain native policy | Existing cap/lifetime and loot deletion | Old loot opportunities disappear during rapid killing | L | Known loss is visible behavior, not a preservation implementation | Lowest additional cost; corpse cap is imperfect |
| More visible corpses | Raise cap/lifetime globally; separate horde cap/lifetime with a propagated ownership tag | Larger looting window and battlefield remains | L global; M horde-only | Global setting affects ambient bodies. Static body records currently lack the original horde-source flag | More GPU instances, colliders/interactions, saved records and proximity scans |
| Choose eviction order | Oldest; empty-first then oldest; protect unopened/nonempty until a second limit or expiry | Different priority for preserving useful bodies | M | Protection alone cannot guarantee both bounded retention and no loot loss; fallback behavior must be chosen. Open looting UI needs safe handling | Sorting/scanning at eviction; protected backlog can grow without a second bound |
| Leave loot after visual removal | Persistent lightweight marker/container per body or local cluster | Loot remains accessible after the model disappears | H | New persistent IDs, position/floating-origin handling, source tags, save/load and exactly-once transfer. Existing interaction/owner-data architecture supports it, but no ready-made corpse-to-cache API was found | Frees corpse rendering; marker/container count and item data still need bounds |
| Aggregate loot | Combine into a horde cache or several area caches | Easier bulk collection; loses individual corpse locations | H | Need item/grid overflow rules, preserve non-stackable metadata, and handle cache lifetime and relocation | Fewer physical objects; serialized inventory and loot generation still grow with kills |
| Drop on ground | Drop already-generated contents; extend with a non-UI generator for unopened corpses | Physical item piles, subject to expiry | M for generated; H for all | Existing `Drop_Storage_Items_On_Ground` only handles existing owner slots. It does not roll unopened bodies. Current dropped-item path destroys objects after 1,800 seconds and provides no save persistence in the inspected path | Per-stack objects, physics, indicators and synchronous icon loads make mass dropping costly |
| When to create unopened loot | Keep an unopened logical claim and roll when collected; roll at death; roll at eviction | Retains delayed-roll behavior, fixes rewards at kill time, or fixes them when displaced | H when decoupled | Current generator is nested in loot-window setup. Moving the roll changes perk/location/setting timing unless those inputs are explicitly captured | Lazy claims defer work; eager rolls can produce kill/eviction spikes and larger saves |

These policies can be combined, for example a visible-body cap with a separately bounded cache. That is an available combination, not a selected design. Retaining orphaned owner records without an interaction object does **not** preserve accessible loot. Suppressing the entire smashed-body event also risks unrelated cleanup.

## 4. Performance constraints and scaling limits

This machine reports a Ryzen 7 9800X3D, RTX 4070 Ti, and approximately 61.6 GiB usable physical memory. Hardware names cannot establish a safe zombie count. **No FPS, frame-time, memory-per-zombie or maximum viable population has been measured.** Existing installed mods also affect any future baseline.

| Inspected system | Consequence for larger hordes | Supported control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pathfinding and per-agent updates | Focused agents can request paths at roughly one-second intervals; movement, grounding, attack and collision work continues more often. GPU instancing does not move this work off the CPU. [E2,E7] | Cap living/focused agents; pace arrivals; stronger enemies at smaller counts |
| Sound and death broadcasts | Noise processing scans the global AI registry. Over the focus threshold it allocates an array and maintains a sorted candidate list, with shifting costs that can grow roughly with population times selected count. Death/frenzy notifications also fan out across registered zombies. [E2] | Separate focus admission if chosen; avoid unnecessarily large registries. Rewriting sound selection is a later optimization requiring behavior-equivalence tests |
| Spawn validation and loading | Raycast retry loops can be expensive around obstructed bases. New groups load Addressables and GPU managers. Shared flags prevent simply launching parallel factories safely. [E1] | Candidate/time budgets per frame, retry backoff, serial queue, bounded optional preloading |
| Damage, ragdolls and destruction | Reactions can leave GPU animation for CPU animation/physics. Ragdoll conversion is delayed. Building destruction requests navigation-graph updates. [E3,E7] | Bound simultaneous living/death pressure; tune conversion carefully; retain compatible native GPU transitions |
| Bodies and loose loot | Static body proximity/expiry checks traverse retained data. Dropped stacks create individual objects; caches reduce object count but not all inventory data. [E4,E6] | Independent corpse, cache and loose-item budgets with explicit overflow behavior |
| Long sessions and saves | Unlimited replacements can still produce unlimited retained records even with low living concurrency | Finite retention/expiry/aggregation policies; measure save size and load time |

**Calculated bounds, not benchmarks:** a serial 0.1-second delay allows at most roughly 10 new spawns per scaled second before asset loading, frame scheduling or invalid positions. From the first spawn, filling 60/120/240 slots takes at least approximately 5.9/11.9/23.9 seconds on that path. Replacement pressure cannot maintain a target when kills exceed effective spawn throughput.

Under simplified oldest-first steady churn, 300 available corpse slots at 5 kills/second represent about **60 seconds** of remains; at 10 kills/second, **30 seconds**. With 50 slots those become 10 and 5 seconds. Existing corpses, hidden bodies, per-prefab exceptions and delayed conversion make actual retention differ. Longer hordes increase this pressure even if only 32 zombies are alive at once.

**What remains necessary to establish a practical ceiling:** a disposable instrumented run in a copied test world, collecting CPU/GPU frame time (median/p95/p99), path requests, spawn/position-search time, allocations, simultaneous ragdolls, active/static saved bodies, loot objects and save/load duration. Test open terrain and a destructible defended base, warm and cold prefab loads, sustained kills and simultaneous explosions. Population steps such as 32, 60, 120 and 240 are experimental points, **not safe presets**. Compare the existing plugin set with a controlled baseline; do not attribute another mod's costs to this controller. No such runtime run was performed during this research.

Verified prefab samples: Desert group 01 has five regular 100-HP variants. Mossy Forest group 03 includes a 250-HP large non-boss at weight 1 and four 100-HP variants at weight 3 each. A large model is therefore not necessarily a boss. [E11]

The loaded world's mutant coefficients are +10 HP, +1 attack and +1 block-damage per level increment. Boss attack/block growth receives a separate 10x factor. Ambient spawning also gives bosses 10x HP growth, but the horde factory inspected here does not apply that boss HP factor. A boss option must decide whether to retain that horde behavior or explicitly alter it. [E1,E8,E9]

Distance, wave and nearby-base scaling are already used; they are not wholly unused progression systems. Days and character progression are additional available inputs. New gear scores, total-world base valuation, or coordinated siege planning would require new definitions and considerably more code than exposing an existing field.

## 6. Feasibility tiers, experiments and gaps

**Small implementation scope with potentially large player effect:** configurable total/concurrency/refill pacing, native composition weights, scoped HP/damage multipliers, and event-start progression snapshots. These are candidates, not priorities or selected behavior. The complete core lifecycle remains M even when individual knobs are L.

**More substantial later candidates:** exact mid-event restoration, protected/aggregated persistent loot, independent AI admission, tested boss adapters, broad resistance coverage and measured adaptive population reduction. Adaptive caps can use frame-time feedback, but create changing difficulty and require smoothing, floors and user-selected response behavior.

**Technically poor or unsupported shortcuts:** “infinite” as a huge native loop count; unrestricted simultaneous async factories; raising population without reconciling the focus selector; arbitrary raw `Destroy` of registered NPCs; keeping every corpse forever; dropping every unopened corpse's loot through an API that only handles existing items; pretending GPU instancing eliminates AI cost. New flying/spitting classes or coordinated siege AI were not established by the inspected assets/code and are outside the supported option set.

Disposable investigation results:

- Decoded the installed encrypted world bundle using the installed `UIResource` decoder, then inspected it with locally isolated UnityPy 1.25.3 and TypeTreeGeneratorAPI 0.0.10. Confirmed ten relevant manager records and their `Mgr_Hub` references. Fifty-six unrelated records could not resolve dependencies from the isolated decoded copy; they are not evidence for absent features. The targeted manager records parsed successfully.
- Inspected base-terrain, desert and forest prefab data. Forest yielded 48 targeted records with no parse errors. No prefab was spawned in-game.
- Read compressed native configuration, horde and corpse saves without writing to the game. Recorded DLL hashes in `.local/expanded-hordes/verification.json`.
- Refreshed the source index after decompilation. `dotnet build HumanHostMods.slnx -c Release` passed with **0 warnings, 0 errors**. This builds existing HelloHost, not Expanded Hordes and not a runtime prototype.

Reproduction scripts: `.local/expanded-hordes/decode_world.ps1`, `inspect_assets.py`, `prefab_summary.py`, `save_and_hash_probe.py`. `inspect_assets.py` accepts installed asset paths; `prefab_summary.py forest-assets.json` joins prefab references to `Zombie_Input`/`Char_Status` by GameObject path ID. Decoded bundle and extracts stay ignored. Tooling reference: [UnityPy's official repository](https://github.com/K0lb3/UnityPy).

Unresolved technical questions: measured CPU/GPU limits and memory growth; runtime boss-group adapter compatibility; no-loss item generation outside the loot UI; exact transition behavior for caves/scene changes; interactions with installed combat/loot/pause plugins; consistent slot save ordering and crash recovery; any UI ModMenu adapter; and all runtime behavior of a future patch. These are explicitly unverified, not blockers to choosing the product behavior.

## 7. Decisions requested before production implementation

1. **Limits:** fixed budget value or unlimited; start schedule; custom nighttime end or vanilla dawn. What should happen when loading after a missed start? Should invalid daytime settings be rejected or clamped to the valid night window?
2. **Pressure:** living horde target; horde-only or combined population accounting; refill delay or pulses; AI allowance and ambient priority. If desired counts cannot be reached, should the event simply continue below target?
3. **Termination:** leave, immediately remove or grace-period survivors at the first enabled limit. Retain native flee/death behavior, or change replenishment around it? Vanilla dawn remains the outer boundary for spawning.
4. **Persistence:** retain native reload behavior, close its zero-survivor continuation gap, or also preserve survivor HP. Apply changed settings next event or immediately?
5. **Loot:** accept native disappearance, retain more bodies, preserve logical loot after body removal, aggregate caches or physical drops. Choose visible retention, loot retention/overflow, and when unopened loot is rolled.
6. **Optional strength:** which composition/boss/stat controls, if any; progression inputs and curves; inherited versus normalized rewards. Do you want fewer/stronger profiles or adaptive caps evaluated?
7. **Interface and validation:** config only or active-event HUD; desired frame-time/FPS target and hardware range for the disposable benchmark. No safe population preset has yet been established.
